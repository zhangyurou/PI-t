Robot trajectory planning serves as a critical bridge between perception and execution. Although data-driven methods based on reinforcement learning and imitation learning enable end-to-end trajectory generation, they often neglect the physical properties of robots, leading to issues such as unsmooth trajectories, jerky motions during execution, and a lack of physical consistency and reliability. To address these challenges, we proposes a physics-informed plug-and-play module (denoted as $\boldsymbol{\pi_t}$), designed to perform dynamics-aware optimization on trajectories generated by diffusion models or other generative policies. With a unique modular design, the proposed module embeds the Lagrangian dynamics of the robot into the trajectory optimization process without modifying the original model architecture, thereby effectively enhancing the physical consistency and smoothness of the trajectories. Experimental results demonstrate that the $\boldsymbol{\pi_t}$ module outperforms existing methods in the vast majority of tasks, improving trajectory smoothness while maintaining or even increasing task success rates. This study aims to expand the application of robotic arm dynamics priors in trajectory planning and imitation learning, improve the rationality and generalization of generative strategies, and provide a new paradigm for future research in robot trajectory planning.

Main Contributions

  • We innovatively use the dynamics prior of the robotic arm as physical information to refine the trajectories generated by the diffusion model, making them more consistent with physical properties.
  • By incorporating a pre-trained model to optimize the generated trajectories, we realize a plug-and-play module, making deployment easier.
  • Based on the existing dataset, we collect the joint information of the robotic arm and compute the corresponding torque, thus increasing the original dataset to support PINN's training.
